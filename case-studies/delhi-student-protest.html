KEY INSIGHT

Negative sentiment on the Government was running at 3.2× the intensity of the Opposition — indicating where public pressure was concentrating.

01

Disproportionate concentration

Narrative pressure was not evenly distributed. It was heavily concentrated on the Government side, particularly around the Education Ministry.

02

Asymmetric accountability

While the Opposition carried only 12% negative narrative, the Government was absorbing 38% — a pattern associated with rising institutional decision pressure.

03

Sustained pressure

The imbalance had been building across the monitoring window rather than appearing as a sudden spike, indicating sustained narrative pressure.

04 THE ASSESSMENT

How Prism interpreted the signal.

Prism's assessment was not derived from raw negative sentiment alone. It was built around the distribution and concentration of narrative pressure across specific entities.

01

Narrative pressure ≠ raw sentiment

A high overall negative score can represent diffuse dissatisfaction. Concentrated pressure on a specific institution indicates something more specific: rising accountability and decision urgency.

02

Entity-level concentration

By tracking narratives at the entity level, Prism surfaces patterns hidden by aggregated data. The 3.2× imbalance between Government and Opposition narrative on 24 July was the key signal.

03

Decision pressure threshold

When sustained narrative pressure reaches an inflection point, organisations may respond through communication shifts, policy adjustments or leadership decisions. Prism tracks when pressure is approaching this threshold.

05 ANALYTICAL BOUNDARIES

Intelligence is useful when its limits are clear.

WHAT THE ASSESSMENT WAS
  • A reading of where narrative pressure was concentrated on 24 July.
  • A pattern-based inference that sustained pressure of this magnitude can precede institutional response.
  • An early-warning signal designed to give stakeholders a preparation window.
  • Based entirely on publicly available narrative data.
WHAT THE ASSESSMENT WAS NOT
  • A prediction based on insider knowledge of political decisions.
  • A guarantee of a specific outcome.
  • A conclusion derived from a single data point.
  • A claim of certainty about what would happen next — it was a probabilistic read of where pressure was concentrating, generated in real time.

07 KEY LEARNING

What this case demonstrates.

Five observations from a real-world narrative intelligence signal.

01

Narrative pressure matters more than raw sentiment.

It is not simply how negative the conversation is, but where that negativity is concentrated. Disproportionate pressure on key entities can indicate rising institutional pressure.

02

Imbalance can signal decision pressure.

A significant gap between narratives around competing entities can reveal rising accountability pressure and the likelihood of a corrective response.

03

Early detection creates a time advantage.

Identifying an unusual narrative pattern 24 hours earlier creates a window to prepare, respond or make better-informed decisions.

04

Entity-level analysis drives clarity.

Looking at narratives around specific people or institutions reveals concentrated pressure hidden inside overall sentiment aggregation.

05

Narrative intelligence enables proactive awareness.

Prism helps organisations move from reactive monitoring toward proactive situational awareness — understanding where momentum is building before events unfold.
